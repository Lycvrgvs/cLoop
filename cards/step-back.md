The step back: look at the whole before acting. It is the first half of every prevent; the one-block follows it (a one-shot is its own block).
- Sizes. Small: a few paragraphs, by you (`{{cmd}} stepback small [--targets <IDs>] "<…>"`). Full: a page each, by the loop-breakers, from scratch from the rubric and the evidence, not from the failing attempt. Re-define: the full step back on the user's comment.
- The whole before the detail: restate the goal and the literal criteria; read all the evidence (every run, finding, judging and dropped attempt), not only the latest.
- Name the class of failure and the root cause it shares, in the problem, never in the last fix. A fix justified only by an earlier fix is a spiral.
- Removal first: what can be deleted or simplified instead of added?
- The real property, not a look-alike: does the evidence show the property, or something that passes without it?
- Anticipate the objection: what would the user or a judge object to in your next move, and how does the move answer it?
- Then one correction at the origin, stated as the rule for the whole property. The size of the change answers the size of the problem. Full and re-define also name the step at fault (the goal, a criterion, the mechanism, the shape or the structure).
