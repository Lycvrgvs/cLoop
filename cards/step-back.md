The step back: one method at three sizes, against the defaults that make loops spiral (fixing the detail instead of the whole, adding instead of removing, satisfying the measure instead of the goal). The size of the change answers the size of the problem.
- The whole before the detail: restate the goal and the targets' literal criteria; read all the evidence (every run, finding, judging and dropped attempt), not only the latest.
- Name the class of failure and the root cause it shares, in the problem, never in the last fix. A fix justified only by an earlier fix is a spiral.
- Removal first: what can be deleted or simplified instead of added?
- The real property, not a look-alike: does the evidence show the property, or something that passes without it?
- Anticipate the objection: what would the user or a judge object to in your next move, and how does the move answer it?
- Then one correction at the origin, stated as the rule for the whole property, and its smallest coherent move.
Sizes:
- Small (yours, a few lines; the PREVENT of every one-shot, loop and explore): `{{cmd}} stepback small --targets <IDs> "<the whole, the class and root cause, what to remove, the real property, the one correction>"`. A target that already failed is reproduced on BEST first.
- Full (each loop-breaker's, a few paragraphs, from scratch, in the representation it reasons best in): also names the step at fault (the goal, a criterion, the mechanism, the shape or the structure).
- Re-define (the full step back with the user): the user's comment, the loop-breakers' re-plan, the user's approval before anything is built.
